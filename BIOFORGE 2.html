01 / CELL LAB

Cells are dynamic machines.

Stem-cell state

Cell fate emerges from interacting genetic programs, extracellular signals, metabolism and mechanical context.

dX/dt = f(X, signals, nutrients, mechanics)

Organoids

Three-dimensional cellular models reproduce selected features of organs and development, while remaining simplified systems.

Mechanosensing

Cells can detect stiffness, tension, shear and deformation and translate those inputs into biochemical responses.

02 / GENOME LAB

The information layer.

CRISPR

Targeted editing

CRISPR-associated systems use guide-directed recognition to target nucleic-acid sequences; the resulting edit depends on cellular repair and context.

target + guide + nuclease → repair
NEXT GEN

Base & prime editing

Newer architectures aim to make more controlled sequence changes and broaden the types of edits possible.

REGULATION

Epigenetics

Gene activity can change through regulatory states without necessarily changing the DNA sequence itself.

Educational mode: this section explains mechanisms and research directions, not operational protocols for editing human cells, embryos or creating harmful biological systems.

07 / FRONTIER RESEARCH

Where the field is moving.

Organoid-on-chip

Microfluidics, organoids and mechanical cues can create more realistic experimental models for disease and therapeutics.

In-situ regeneration

Research aims to engineer environments that guide endogenous repair rather than assembling an entire tissue externally.

Multiscale simulation

molecule → cell → tissue → organ

Connecting scales is a major modeling challenge.

Adaptive prosthetics

Richer biological signals and machine learning may enable more responsive assistive systems.

Biohybrid systems

Research explores combinations of living cells and engineered components.

Human enhancement

Potential augmentation raises questions of safety, reversibility, consent, equity and governance.

08 / ETHICS & BIOSECURITY

Power needs boundaries.

Somatic vs heritable

Somatic edits affect treated individuals; heritable changes can affect future generations and therefore carry different governance concerns.

Dual use

The same scientific knowledge can support medicine or be misused. Responsible education separates mechanism from operational harm.

Governance

WHO emphasizes oversight, international collaboration, registries, education and responsible governance for human genome editing.
